Personal Statement

Who I am, why I tutor, and what I believe about teaching.

My name is Tsholofelo Celeste Tshwenyane, and I am a student at Walter Sisulu University, pursuing a Diploma in ICT in Applications Development. My academic journey has developed my interest in software development, programming, logical thinking, and the use of technology to solve problems.

Beyond my academic responsibilities, I value leadership, teamwork, communication, accountability, and continuous learning. These values have influenced the way I approach my responsibilities as a student and as a tutor.

My decision to become involved in tutoring was motivated by the opportunity to share knowledge, support fellow students, and contribute to their academic development. Having experienced the challenges of learning programming myself, I understand that technical subjects require patience, practice, and a willingness to learn from mistakes. This understanding has encouraged me to approach tutoring with empathy and to explain concepts in ways that students can understand.

My teaching philosophy is centred on the belief that students should not simply memorise information or copy solutions. Instead, they should develop an understanding of the concepts, the logic behind them, and how to apply their knowledge independently. I aim to create a supportive learning environment where students feel comfortable asking questions, participating in discussions, and learning from their mistakes.

Through tutoring, I have the opportunity to contribute to the academic development of others while strengthening my own communication, facilitation, leadership, and problem-solving skills. I regard tutoring as a shared learning experience in which both the tutor and the students continue to grow.

My goal is to become a more effective, reflective, and student-centred tutor while continuing to develop my technical knowledge and professional skills.

Tutorial Delivery

How I planned and ran tutorials, and how I supported students' learning.

My approach to tutorial delivery is centred on making learning interactive, practical, and accessible. In programming, students need more than an explanation of syntax; they need to understand the reasoning behind a solution and how to apply the same principles to unfamiliar problems.

I aim to structure tutorial activities so that students can progress from understanding a concept to applying it independently.

My tutorial approach

  1. Preparation

    Before a tutorial, I review the relevant content and identify the key concepts and learning objectives. Preparation helps me organise the session and anticipate areas where students may need additional explanation.

  2. Introduction and explanation

    I introduce the topic, explain important terminology, and connect new information to concepts students may already understand.

  3. Demonstration

    Where appropriate, I use worked examples to demonstrate how a programming concept can be applied. I explain the reasoning behind each step rather than presenting only the final solution.

  4. Guided practice

    Students attempt exercises while receiving guidance when necessary. I encourage them to explain their reasoning and identify errors instead of immediately providing answers.

  5. Questions and clarification

    I respond to students' questions and use alternative examples where a concept remains unclear. I aim to make the learning environment welcoming and supportive.

  6. Review and reflection

    At the end of a tutorial, I review the main concepts and reflect on the difficulties encountered, the effectiveness of my explanations, and possible improvements for future sessions.

My role as a tutor

My role is to support students in developing understanding, confidence, and independence. I recognise that students learn at different paces and that effective tutoring requires patience, flexibility, and an awareness of individual learning needs.

I aim to guide students towards solutions rather than making them dependent on the tutor. Ultimately, I want students to leave a tutorial better equipped to approach similar problems on their own.

Reflections & Lessons Learnt

An honest look at my experience, challenges, achievements and next steps.

My tutoring experience has provided an opportunity to reflect on my academic knowledge, communication skills, and ability to support other students. It has helped me recognise that effective tutoring involves more than understanding the subject matter. It also requires preparation, patience, adaptability, and an understanding of how people learn.

Key lessons

Knowing is not explaining

Understanding a concept myself does not automatically mean I can explain it well. I need to consider the learner's perspective, break ideas into manageable steps, and choose helpful examples.

Patience and flexibility

Students approach problems differently and may need more than one explanation. A tutor should encourage questions and treat mistakes as opportunities for learning.

Preparation and reflection

A well-structured tutorial makes it easier to communicate objectives, manage time, and provide relevant examples. Reflecting afterwards shows what worked and what to improve.

Challenges and areas for improvement

During the year, I experienced a few challenges. First, managing a wide range of prior programming experience within the same tutorial group some students needed more time on fundamentals while others were ready to move ahead quickly. Second, maintaining engagement when the material became more abstract, particularly around object-oriented concepts, where students could follow the syntax but struggled to see the purpose. Third, balancing time ensuring I covered the required material while still leaving enough space for questions and for students to work through problems themselves.

These experiences have encouraged me to consider how I can improve my tutorial planning, student engagement, and assessment of learning outcomes. In future sessions, I would like to make greater use of interactive exercises, short knowledge checks, and opportunities for students to explain their reasoning.

A moment that taught me something

A student was stuck on arrays. Specifically, why array[0] is the first element, and why an array of size 10 only goes from 0 to 9. I first explained it the normal way zero-based indexing, memory offsets. That didn't help at all, it just made it sound more complicated and arbitrary. So I explained it differently. I said think of an array like a row of lockers, and the index isn't the locker number, it's how many steps you have to take from the start. array[0] means 0 steps from the start you're already at the first locker. array[3] means 3 steps forward. And if there are 10 lockers, you can take 0 to 9 steps and still be inside the row. If you take 10 steps, you've walked past the last locker that's why array[10] is out of bounds. That made sense to them right away.What I learned from that was to stop explaining what the rule is and start explaining why the rule exists in physical terms. Once they had the mental picture of walking down a row, they didn't need to memorize "arrays start at 0" anymore.

My overall reflection

Overall, tutoring has been an opportunity for both contribution and personal development. It has allowed me to support other students while strengthening skills that are relevant to my future career in technology. I have come to appreciate the importance of sharing knowledge responsibly, communicating clearly, and remaining willing to learn from every experience.

I recognise that professional growth is an ongoing process. I intend to use the lessons gained from this experience to become a more effective tutor, a stronger communicator, and a more reflective professional.
